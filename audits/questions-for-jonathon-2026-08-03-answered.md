**2. The botanical name — I need you to confirm this one.**

Your site currently contradicts itself. The Master Plants page says Yagé-ocó is
*Diplopterys cabrerana*, "originally from the Wambiza tribe." Neil Logan's article on the same
site says it is *Diplopterys longialata* var. *huambisa*, and argues at length that *cabrerana*
is the misidentification. (Wambiza and Huambisa are the same word.)

On the recording you said *longialata*, so the new site says:

> **Yagé-ocó · Yají** — *Diplopterys longialata* (Huambisa type)
> Commonly published as *Diplopterys cabrerana*; the two are closely similar and are
> frequently conflated.

Two things to confirm:
- Is *longialata* the identification you stand behind?
- I have **dropped "var. huambisa"**. Huambisa is a people and a trade name — it is not a
  validly published botanical variety, and several vendors attach it to *cabrerana*. Writing it
  in italics as formal taxonomy is the one thing a botanist could call an error. Saying
  "Huambisa type" carries the same meaning and is safe. Are you happy with that?

I have also corrected six spellings on the Master Plants page: *Brunflesia* → **Brunfelsia**,
*Dacryoydes* → **Dacryodes**, *Grias nueberthii* → **neuberthii**, *Hymenea* → **Hymenaea**,
*Tabernaemontana sanaho* → **sananho**, and *Ocimum sanctum* → **tenuiflorum** (the newer
accepted name). Shout if any of those was deliberate.

One more: your articles spell the leaf **chakruna** twice and **chacruna** once. I've
standardised on **chacruna** because that is what people search for, with "also written
chakruna" noted on the page. Say the word if chakruna is your spelling and I'll flip it.
