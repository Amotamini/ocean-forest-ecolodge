## THE LODGE

Ocean Forest Ecolodge is a rainforest and ocean discovery center and nature sanctuary on
Playa Rincón de San Josecito, a secluded mile-long beach just south of Drake Bay, on the
Osa Peninsula, Costa Rica. It borders the north side of Corcovado National Park — about
6 km away. It sits on a 15-acre permaculture property right on the beach, with fruit and
food orchards, ethnobotanical tea and aromatic herb gardens. Founded in 2003.

There is no road along this beach. The last stretch of every arrival is a walk along the sand.

Buildings:
- **La Choza Lodge** — open-air reception, kitchen and dining area; the lodge's central hub,
  and where the internet reception zone is.
- **La Choza Lounge & Library** — stuccoed bamboo walls, local art, skylights, guide books on
  wildlife, birds and ocean life, plus a map of the grounds.
- **The Lapa Lapa Lodge** — a three-storey pagoda-like building rising 14 m / 46 ft. No living
  trees were cut for it; built from reclaimed hardwoods, fallen timbers, bamboo and recycled
  materials.
  - 1st floor **Dharma Hall** — 150 m² / 1,614 ft² of tiled floor, cool through the day, used
    for gatherings, conferences, yoga and movement arts.
  - 2nd floor **Yoga Shala** — polished hardwood floor nicknamed "foot candy", under a 6 m /
    19 ft parabolic roof. Two instructor/guest rooms share an en-suite bath and open onto a
    large front porch. Yoga mats, on-site balsa blocks and straps are kept here.
  - 3rd floor **The Cupola** — up in the rainforest canopy; polished hardwood for yoga,
    massage or meditation; boundless sunsets over the Pacific.
- **Turtle Island Labyrinth** — in front of the main lodge, dedicated to endangered marine
  sea turtles.
- **White Hawk Nature Trail** — climbs behind the lodge to an ocean lookout with panoramic
  views of San Josecito Beach, the Pacific and Caño Island; an ethnobotanical garden and a
  meditation lodge with hammocks sit above the lookout.

Facts that override any older text: **ten rooms**, **maximum 32 guests**, **three meals a day
included**.

## FOOD

Farm-to-table cuisine. Three meals a day, made from what the garden and the sea offer that
morning. Fresh fruit juices, medicinal herbs, detox teas. No menus — every meal changes with
the tides and the season. Local fishermen, the lodge garden and the jungle itself supply the
kitchen. Fish is sustainably caught and local only.

Careful: the website no longer advertises an open-air dining room or all-day self-serve
filtered water, coffee and tea to nightly guests. That copy was removed on 2026-08-03. All-day
coffee and tea service is currently offered **only as part of a retreat package** (/retreats).
Do not promise it to an independent guest. If asked about drinks outside mealtimes, say fresh
juices come with meals and hand the question to the lodge.

Four diet options:
- **Vegetarian** — legumes, seeds, fresh vegetables, whole grains and tropical fruits;
  complete plant-based protein.
- **Vegan** — fully plant-based menus, free of animal products: hearty bowls, vibrant salads,
  slow-cooked stews and bright sauces.
- **Gluten-free** — built around naturally gluten-free ingredients like rice, roots, beans,
  vegetables and fruits; many dishes adapted on request.
- **Omnivore** — plant-based sides paired with sustainably caught local seafood, free-range
  meats and eggs.

Allergies and special diets are carefully accommodated. Because the lodge is remote and menus
are seasonally driven, guests should share dietary preferences **at least 48 hours before
arrival**. Last-minute special requests may need up to one week's notice and depend on
availability. (The website states the 48-hour window only; it no longer names a check-in form.)

### Ocean Discovery

**The Island – Caño Island.** Caño Island can be seen right in front of the lodge and is
reached by a 40-minute boat ride. Known to the local indigenous Brunka people as "The Floating
Butterfly". Declared a marine reserve in 1976; 790 acres in size, protecting an oceanic region
of over 17,000 acres. Encounters with dolphins, pseudo orcas, whales, sea turtles, sea snakes
and large schools of fish. Once an important ceremonial and burial site of the Diquis
civilization; rock mortars and one ancient petro-sphere remain. Island trails are closed by
the park service — only snorkeling and scuba diving are allowed.
Book snorkeling: https://www.wetravel.com/trips/cano-island-snorkelling-tour-ocean-forest-ecolodge-69074848
Book diving: https://www.wetravel.com/trips/cano-island-diving-tour-ocean-forest-ecolodge-22781271

**Scuba Diving at Caño Island — $165.** The most epic scuba diving near mainland Costa Rica.
Departs 7:00 AM, returns by 1 PM for lunch. Includes equipment and a certified PADI guide.
40-minute boat ride. Divers meet pelagic and Pacific reef fish, two-metre white-tipped reef
sharks, manta and lesser-devil rays, free-swimming moray eels, and marine turtles such as the
juvenile Hawksbill; occasionally a whale shark.
Book: https://www.wetravel.com/trips/cano-island-diving-tour-ocean-forest-ecolodge-22781271

**Snorkeling at Caño Island — $99.** The most epic snorkeling near mainland Costa Rica, where
coral shelves harbour rich oceanic life. Departs 7:30 AM, returns by 1 PM for lunch. Includes
equipment and a qualified guide. 40-minute boat ride. Big eye jacks, angel fish, parrot fish,
crocodile needlefish, puffer and cornet fishes, Moorish idols, sea turtles, eagle rays, moray
eels and white tip sharks. Whales are often seen breaching June through December.
Book: https://www.wetravel.com/trips/cano-island-snorkelling-tour-ocean-forest-ecolodge-69074848

**Dolphin and Whale Encounters.** Departs 8 AM, includes lunch, returns between 1 and 2:30 PM.
Two distinct populations of Humpback whales breed here: the southern population arrives July–
September, the northern population October–December. Whale watching is seasonal, July to
December. Pseudo Orcas and Pilot Whales live here year-round, as do Pacific spotted, bottlenose
and spinner dolphins.
Book: https://www.wetravel.com/trips/dolphin-whales-encounter-ocean-forest-ecolodge-57401510

**Surf Tour at Rio Claro.** A point break in a gorgeous, remote location; great for experienced
and beginning surfers. Includes board and instructor. Board rentals are $15 per day. A
45-minute walk north of the lodge. Best at mid tides. Half day, and only possible when the
surf is up. In September and March the wave demands a strong surfer; the rest of the time it
suits beginners.
Ask about it on WhatsApp: https://wa.me/50687379416

### Rainforest Discovery

**Corcovado National Park.** The crown jewel of Costa Rica's national parks, known as "the
Little Amazon by the Ocean". National Geographic ranks it among the top ten nature
destinations on Earth. It protects over 120,000 acres of lowland tropical rainforest and is
surrounded by the even larger Golfo Dulce forest reserve. Home to jaguar and five other wild
cat species, all four Costa Rican monkey species, peccaries, tapirs, forest deer, curassow,
guans and hundreds of bird species. Three destinations are recommended: Sirena Ranger Station,
San Pedrillo Ranger Station and the Llorona wilderness area.
Ask about it on WhatsApp: https://wa.me/50687379416

**Corcovado – Sirena Ranger Station — $120.** Among the most remote and wildlife-filled spots
in Costa Rica, reached only by boat. Departs 6:30 AM, back at the lodge by 3:30 PM. Breakfast
at 6:00 AM, then a walk to San Josecito beach and a boat ride down the coast, passing the
Llorona waterfall which cascades over 100 feet to the beach. Tapir, both Costa Rican peccary
species, all four monkey species and forest cats are frequently encountered. Picnic lunch;
one or two trails depending on the group's stamina. For groups of 8 or more, the lodge's own
specialised guides lead the tour.
Book: https://www.wetravel.com/trips/corcovado-national-park-sirena-station-ocean-forest-ecolodge-75443139

**Corcovado – San Pedrillo — $110.** The tallest trees in the new-world tropics, reaching over
190 feet. Departs 6:30 AM, back at the lodge by 3:30 PM. Breakfast at 6:00 AM, then a
20-minute boat ride. Walking distance 5–10 km (3–6 miles). The trail winds down to a large
waterfall and follows the creek to the ocean; a second trail leads to the Rio Pargo through
gallery rainforest. Picnic lunch; departure from the park at 2:30 PM. The shorter boat ride
makes this the gentler Corcovado option. For groups of 8 or more, the lodge's own specialised
guides lead the tour.
Book: https://www.wetravel.com/trips/corcovado-national-park-san-pedrillo-station-ocean-forest-ecolodge-28113992

**Goddess Jacuzzi.** Waterfalls and bubble-filled pools at the Rio Claro headwaters, reached
through towering primary rainforest. 9 AM to 4 PM, picnic lunch at the pools, includes round
trip taxi fare. A 20-minute beach walk to the local school house, a 4×4 jeep taxi to the
village of Los Planes, then on foot to the pools. Two waterfalls splash into a large swimming
hole with a roughly 15-foot jump for the adventurous.
Ask about it on WhatsApp: https://wa.me/50687379416

## GETTING HERE

Three routes. The last stretch is always a walk along the beach.

**1. By boat — all seasons, and our recommendation.**
First get to the river town of Sierpe: private taxi $70 (about 6 hours from San José), or
public bus $20 (about 7 hours). Then the boat from Sierpe to San Josecito beach: **$30 on the
11:30 am boat, $40 on the 4:00 pm boat**, about 1.5 hours. Then a 15-minute walk along the
beach to the lodge.
The boat ride is a wildlife tour in itself — down the tranquil Sierpe River through Central
America's largest mangrove forest, with monkeys, caimans, crocodiles and birds, before the
river opens onto the Pacific for a coastal run to San Josecito Beach. Arrive 30 minutes early
to secure your spot. The Sierpe dock is next to Donde Jorge Restaurant, which also has secure
24-hour parking. Always tell the boat operator you are going to San Josecito Beach for Ocean
Forest Ecolodge. Pack light in a backpack (max 15 kg / 33 lbs), keep electronics in waterproof
bags, and wear water sandals for the beach landing.

**2. By car — dry season only (December–April).**
A 4×4 is required; the roads are unpaved and cross two shallow rivers. Park in the secure
parking area at San Josecito Rural School, then walk 15 minutes along the beach to the lodge.
Heavy rain can make the river crossings impassable, so always check the weather. Share your
estimated arrival time so a staff member can meet you and help with luggage.

**3. By air — all seasons.**
Fly San José to Drake Bay (about 45 minutes), then a 4×4 taxi, **$70 per vehicle**, about
45 minutes. Book flights with SANSA well in advance; the lodge can help — write to
eli@oceanforest.org. Send your itinerary so the ground transfer can be arranged; Drake Bay
Airport is rural and taxis are not readily available on the spot. Then the 15-minute beach walk.

Porter service is available on request via the reservation form — horseback or motorised quad
transfer for luggage.

Important: there are **no ATMs** near Ocean Forest, Sierpe or Drake Bay. Bring enough cash for
transfers and local expenses. Colones and US dollars are both accepted, and services can be
paid online via PayPal.

## SEASONS AND WEATHER

- **Green Season: May–November.** Lush landscapes and refreshing rains; September and October
  see the highest rainfall.
- **Dry Season: December–April.** Abundant sunshine; March and April are the warmest months.

Tropical warmth year-round: days average mid-90s °F (34 °C), nights mid-70s °F (22–23 °C),
humidity 68–90%.

## WHAT TO PACK

Essentials: head lamp or flashlight; sunhat, beach towel and flip flops; reef-safe sunscreen
and bug repellent; day pack and reusable water bottle; bathing suit and lightweight quick-dry
clothes; biodegradable toiletries; sturdy hiking shoes. A portable fold-up umbrella or
lightweight rain poncho from May through November. A pullover or light sweater for San José,
which can be chilly at night.

Optional: sunglasses, water shoes for boats and hiking, a lightweight windbreaker or long
sleeve shirt for boat rides, camera and binoculars, snorkel and mask (the lodge has some sets
and plenty of fins), a journal.

Luggage: a backpack is far better than a suitcase because the last leg is on foot — max 15 kg
/ 33 lbs. Local flights allow 11 kg / 25 lbs. Porter service is available if a suitcase is
what you have. There are remarkably few bugs here, but bring repellent if you are prone to
bites.

## THE SHALA AND RETREATS

The Lapa Lapa Lodge is a three-level pagoda at the centre of the property, rising 46 ft / 14 m:
the Dharma Hall on the ground floor (1,614 ft² / 150 m² of cool tiled space for gatherings, workshops
and ceremonies), the hardwood Yoga Shala above it — the "foot candy" floor, fully equipped with
props — and an observatory cupola on top with panoramic views and ocean breezes. The lodge
hosts up to 32 guests in bungalows and suites with private baths, with dedicated facilitator
rooms beside the shala, three meals a day, and an on-site team that handles planning,
transport, scheduling and daily logistics.
