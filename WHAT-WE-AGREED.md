For Eli and Jonathon. Last updated 7 August 2026.

conversation it came from. **If anything here is not what you meant, say so and we change it.**
Nothing is set in stone; the point of writing it down is so we are all looking at the same list

## The shape of the site

**Five pages, in this order: Arriving, Lodging, Experiences, Retreats, About.**
*Agreed in the meeting of 4 August.*

The Gallery is no longer a menu item. It appears at the bottom of every page instead, showing a
different selection each time, so you see photographs everywhere rather than having to go looking
for them.

About was added at the end of that meeting, as a place for the story, the history, the projects,
the blog and the FAQ.

**"Lodging", not "Stay".**
*Changed 4 August, replacing what was agreed on 3 August.*

**The pages hold everything. The home page is a short version of them.**
*Agreed 4 August.*

Eli wanted separate pages with the full detail. Mehdi wanted one page you scroll. Rather than
compromise, we did both in a particular order: each page is written complete, with everything on
it, and then the home page is shortened from those pages. So someone who wants to read gets
everything, and someone who wants to skim never has to open a page to find out what is offered.

Nothing is written twice. If the home page and a page ever disagree, the page is right.

**Wherever you would normally have to click through to read something, it opens where it is
instead.** Rooms, tours, activities, questions. Nobody loses their place, and nobody on a slow
connection waits for a page to load just to read a paragraph.

## Arriving

This is the page that has been costing bookings. Eli: *"a lot of people are saying, oh that is so
far, I'm not going."*

**The three maps stop being shown all at once.**
*Agreed 4 August.*

At the moment three maps sit side by side, which is three questions asked at the same time with
nothing saying which one applies to you. Now it works in three layers:

1. **Where is this place?** One map. Always visible.
2. **How do I get there?** No map at first. You pick how you are travelling, and only then does
   your route appear. The other three routes are not shown, because they are not yours.
3. **What happens when I land?** Always shown, because every route ends the same way.

**The words come before the map.** The single most common confusion is which drop-off point you
arrive at and which way you then walk. A map is a poor way to say that, so each route card says it
in a sentence first:

> Boat from Sierpe → you land at San Josecito **Beach** → walk **south** 20 minutes, ocean on your
> right.
>
> Car, taxi or plane → you are dropped at San Josecito **School** → walk **north** 20 minutes,
> ocean on your left.

**The walk is described as 20 minutes, not 15.**
*Decided 4 August.*

The old site says 15 in one place and "15 to 20" in another. We chose the longer number
deliberately: this is exactly the figure that becomes a complaint when someone is carrying a
suitcase in the heat. Better to be generous and have people pleasantly surprised.

**Four ways to get here**, each with its own card: boat from Sierpe (recommended, all year), bus
plus boat (budget, all year), driving (dry season only), and flying to Drake Bay (dry season only).

**The honest warning goes above the routes, not buried below them.** The boat from Sierpe is the
safest arrival and you do not need a car here. Driving means a 4x4 and two river crossings, and in
rain we cannot guarantee either direction. Every route ends with a walk on sand, so pack light.

This is deliberately said up front rather than hidden. Someone who is surprised by it on arrival
writes a bad review; someone who was told beforehand feels looked after.

**The Departing section, the Helpful Contacts and the What to Pack list are all carried over from
oceanforest.org**, where they were already written. We did not rewrite them.

## Lodging

**Food lives on this page, not on a page of its own, and there is no menu.**
*Agreed 4 August.*

Three meals a day, made from what the garden and the sea offer that morning. Every meal changes
with the season. Allergies and special diets accommodated with 48 hours' notice.

The reasoning: not having a menu was being treated as a weakness to apologise for. It is better as
a signature. Guests sit down and food arrives, and nobody has to choose. That is a real thing to
offer, not a gap.

**The amenities are shown as photographs, not icons.**
*Eli was explicit about this on 4 August.* The five photographs from your own site are used.

**An honest note about the nature, near the top of the page.**
*Agreed 4 August.*

This is one of the most thriving ecosystems on the planet. The rooms are bugproof, but life gets
in. Rather than being defensive when a guest mentions an insect, the page says so first, and frames
it as the place working exactly as it should.

**The hold harmless agreement and the cancellation policy both appear on this page**, next to the
rates, because that is where people look for them and it is why some guests book through
Booking.com instead.

**The hand-drawn property map is used, not the redrawn one.** The redrawn version is prettier but
its labels cannot be read at the size it is shown.
